PROJECT CONTEXT
================================================================================

EchoSafe is a wearable, around-the-ear sound-alert device for people who are
deaf or hard of hearing, shaped like a Shokz-style bone-conduction headset.
It listens for specific target sounds (currently: horns, bells, gunshots,
sirens — plus a "noise" background/reject class), classifies them on-device
with an ML model, and fires directional haptic motors so the wearer knows
WHAT sound occurred and WHICH direction it came from.

- 4 MEMS microphones, one per side of the head (front-left/top-left,
  front-right/top-right, rear-left/bottom-left, rear-right/bottom-right),
  used both for ML classification and for direction detection via
  cross-correlation-based TDOA (time-difference-of-arrival) between mic
  pairs — whichever mic(s) a sound reaches first indicate direction; if two
  mics are equidistant the sound arrives at both simultaneously and both
  motors fire together.
- 4 haptic motors (DRV2605 driver ICs via a TCA9548A I2C mux), positioned to
  mirror the mic layout, so activation location communicates direction.
- MCU: ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 (N16R8 variant). This has already been validated as
  the right choice — do NOT propose switching MCUs. The apparent "only 2 I2S
  peripherals but need 4 mics" problem is already solved: each I2S bus runs
  in stereo (L/R) mode with two mics sharing one data line via their L/R
  select pin, so I2S_NUM_0 carries the TOP pair and I2S_NUM_1 carries the
  BOTTOM pair — 4 mics, 2 physical buses, no mux chip required.

THIS IS NOT A GREENFIELD PROJECT. Significant work already exists across
several folders on this machine (paths below). Your first job is
understanding and consolidating that work, NOT starting over.
